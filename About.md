## About

Indigenous Peoples worldwide face persistent health inequities shaped by historical, structural, and systemic barriers to care[^1]. While advances in genomics are increasingly being applied in health research and integrated into clinical practice for personalized medicine and rare disease diagnosis, Indigenous communities worldwide continue to experience limited access to genomic technologies and services[^2][^3]. Many factors contribute to this gap, including a lack of culturally safe care, limited Indigenous health research, inadequate infrastructure, geographic isolation of communities, and mistrust of colonial systems[^4]. 

Barriers to inclusion of Indigenous Peoples in genomic research and healthcare have resulted in a lack of genomic reference data reflective of Indigenous populations. Reference data helps with the interpretation of genetic testing, by distinguishing common variants (usually unrelated to disease) from rare variants (more likely to be disease-causing). Publicly available resources, such as the Genome Aggregation Database (gnomAD)[^5], provide population-level variant frequency data to support interpretation; however, Indigenous Peoples remain underrepresented or absent within these databases worldwide. To support access to genomic technology for Indigenous Peoples, it is imperative to create resources containing population-based variant information, while being committed to maintaining data sovereignty principles recognized in the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples. 

As a response to the lack of genomic health equity for Indigenous peoples, two projects, He Kākano: The Aotearoa Māori Variome (New Zealand) and The Silent Genomes Project Indigenous Background Variant Library (Canada), developed population-specific reference databases under Indigenous governance and guided by principles of Indigenous data sovereignty. ***A Home for Your Variome*** was developed through collaboration between the two projects, to support others in creating population-specific variomes. 

***A Home for Your Variome*** is an open-source set of software modules which can be used to create a population specific genetic variation repository software system for sensitive data that incorporates Indigenous data sovereignty principles. The software, available on GitHub, was developed for use with standard databases and uses Python, Django, and React JS for the web application and can be accessed and adapted by projects with similar goals and commitments to data sovereignty. Its modular design paradigm enables it to be adjusted according to local expertise and community-centered needs. The modules also support needs that arise when engaging with sensitive data, such as auditability of access, appropriate limitations on use, and the ability to integrate existing authentication tools into the application. The web application also includes clinician-focused features including a robust variant search, links to external resources, and transcript effect predictors. 

To access this software system and welcome your variome to its new home, visit our GitHub: https://github.com/wassermanlab/variome
